ECG Patient Matching

Can a model recognize the same person from two ECG recordings—even when one comes from a clinical ECG and the other from an Apple Watch?

This team research project explores unseen-patient ECG similarity using waveform-only data, patient-level contrastive learning, and controlled CNN/GRU experiments.

CASE STUDY

Learning identity from changing ECG waveforms

PROBLEM

ECG identity is not a fixed waveform

The model must recognize the same patient across time and recording devices without relying on demographics or patient IDs.

Python PyTorch InfoNCE CNN GRU AUROC
INPUT

Two Lead-I ECG waveforms are processed independently. The task is not disease diagnosis and not fixed patient-ID classification.

OUTPUT

The model produces a continuous similarity score: higher similarity should indicate stronger evidence that both ECGs came from the same person.

GENERALIZATION

Evaluation focuses on patients not seen during training and includes both clinical-to-clinical and clinical-to-Apple Watch matching.

CORE CHALLENGE

The same person's ECG can change with time, heart rate, rhythm, physiology, signal quality, and acquisition conditions while still representing the same identity.

MY ROLE

Model development, controlled experiments, and failure analysis

MODEL DEVELOPMENT

I developed and evaluated CNN-based beat representations within the team's existing preprocessing, beat-quality, contrastive-learning, batching, and evaluation framework.

CONTROLLED COMPARISON

I compared CNN and GRU representation strategies while keeping the surrounding training setup as consistent as possible, helping isolate architecture effects from pipeline changes.

ABLATION DESIGN

I helped structure experiments that separated the beat encoder from cross-beat sequence modeling rather than treating each neural architecture as a black box.

ERROR ANALYSIS

I investigated difficult same-patient cases and helped shift the research focus toward longitudinal morphology and physiological-state change.

APPROACH

Patient-level contrastive representation learning

Instead of predicting a fixed patient label, the models learn an embedding in which recordings from the same patient should be closer than recordings from different patients.

ECG AClinical or wearable waveform
EncoderCNN / GRU representation model
EmbeddingPatient-specific learned representation
SimilarityContinuous score evaluated with AUROC

Experimental principle: patient-level splitting and controlled comparisons matter as much as architecture. The project explicitly treats leakage checks, subgroup evaluation, and failure diagnosis as part of model development.

KEY FINDING

The device difference was not necessarily the hardest problem

CROSS-DEVICE

CNN-based representations performed especially well in clinical-to-Apple Watch matching, showing that cross-device identity information can remain strong.

LONGITUDINAL

Clinical-to-clinical matching became more difficult for some same-patient recordings separated by longer periods and larger morphology changes.

RESEARCH SHIFT

The central question evolved from “Which architecture wins?” to “How can patient identity remain stable across legitimate within-person ECG changes?”

NEXT HYPOTHESIS

A promising direction is to expose models to more longitudinal views of the same patient rather than relying on one fixed positive pair per patient.

WHAT I LEARNED

Better experiments can matter more than a more complicated network

The most valuable workflow became: build a baseline → run a controlled experiment → inspect failures → form a hypothesis → design the next test. This project strengthened my understanding of representation learning, experimental design, leakage prevention, reproducibility, and model diagnostics.
