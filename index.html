FEATURED PROJECT · BASKETBALL ANALYTICS

Using Possession Data to Redesign Defensive Strategy

National Taiwan University Men’s Basketball

Excel Video Tagging Performance Analysis Decision Support
CONTEXT

Our team faced a significant size and talent disadvantage, and my initial full-court pressure strategy was not producing consistent results. With no budget for commercial analytics software, we needed a practical way to evaluate what was actually working.

MY ROLE

As head coach, I organized and trained a six-member volunteer analysis team, created a manual possession-tagging process, and structured video and scouting observations in Excel for game preparation and strategic review.

ANALYSIS

The data showed a clear defensive gap: opponents scored 1.08 points per possession against our full-court pressure versus 0.92 in the half court. We also found that opponent efficiency dropped from 1.04 PPP when driving right to 0.83 when forced left.

DECISION

I shifted our emphasis toward half-court defense and introduced a simple weak-hand rule for ballhandlers. I then used film and data to explain the changes to players so the strategy could be understood and executed on the court.

BUSINESS ANALYTICS · STATISTICAL MODELING

Exploring What Drives Spotify Track Popularity

Wake Forest MSBA · Team Analytics Project

Excel Descriptive Statistics Hypothesis Testing Regression
QUESTION

Using a large Spotify track dataset, our team examined how audio characteristics such as danceability, energy, loudness, and related features were associated with track popularity.

APPROACH

We cleaned and summarized the data, examined distributions and visual patterns, built probability and inference analyses, and then used multiple linear regression to test how well the available audio features explained popularity.

KEY INSIGHT

After removing tracks with a popularity score of zero, the regression still explained only about 7% of the variation in popularity. Rather than treating the low R² as a failed model, we interpreted it as evidence that audio features alone provide limited predictive power.

Business implication: The project reinforced the importance of model limitations and variable selection. A stronger popularity model would likely require information beyond the audio characteristics available in this dataset.
