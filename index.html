COMPUTER VISION · SPORTS DATA ENGINEERING · IN PROGRESS

NBA Court Vision Tracker

Broadcast video → player and ball positions on the court, in meters · Python / OpenCV / ONNX

Python OpenCV ONNX Runtime Camera Calibration Multi-Object Tracking
PROBLEM

Player-tracking data drives modern team analysis, but optical tracking systems are installed only in professional arenas and their data is rarely public. Broadcast video exists for almost every game. I wanted to find out how much reliable spatial data can be recovered from the broadcast alone.

APPROACH

The pipeline detects players and the ball, estimates the broadcast camera's pan, tilt and zoom frame by frame from the painted court lines, tracks each player, and projects every position onto a top-down court in meters with an attached uncertainty.

KEY DECISION

The biggest risk is a confident but wrong camera estimate, which silently moves every player on the court. I built a quality gate that grades each frame and withholds court coordinates when the fit is doubtful. It is tested on real failure cases, not only synthetic ones.

STATUS & NEXT

The system runs end to end on real broadcasts, including a 113-second multi-camera sequence. Keeping one ID per player through crossings is the main open problem. Human-reviewed annotations are in progress so that tracking accuracy can be measured rather than estimated.

3.3%Wrong camera fits accepted on unseen frames, down from 85%
99%+Main-camera frames with an accepted camera fit
113 sMulti-camera broadcast processed end to end
121Automated tests

Analytical value: Every row of the output states whether a position was observed, interpolated or unknown, so later analysis of spacing, matchups or possessions can work within the data's limits instead of guessing them. A companion tool reads the scoreboard to turn a broadcast into a searchable game timeline.

ECG Patient Matching Across Clinical and Wearable Devices

Unseen-patient recognition with patient-contrastive learning and a four-model CNN/GRU ensemble.

PythonPyTorchInfoNCECNNGRU
THE QUESTION

Can two ECG recordings identify the same unseen person—even if they were captured at different times or with different devices?

THE METHOD

Our team used a K2 score-level ensemble of three independently trained CNNs and one Raw GRU to compare 128-dimensional ECG embeddings.

MY CONTRIBUTION

I developed beat-CNN experiments, supported controlled architecture comparisons and cross-team validation, and investigated longitudinal morphology changes behind difficult matches.

KEY FINDING

Identified longitudinal changes in ECG waveform shape as a major challenge in matching recordings from the same patient over time.

SPORTS DATA ENGINEERING · VALIDATION · IN PROGRESS

NBA Analytics Platform

Every NBA game since 1996-97 → my own possessions, checked game by game · Python / DuckDB / SQL

Python DuckDB SQL Streamlit pytest
PROBLEM

Per-possession metrics such as on/off and RAPM depend on judgment calls that public numbers make silently: when a possession ends, how technical free throws count, who gets credit when a substitution happens between free throws.

APPROACH

I built a play-by-play database for 1996-97 → 2025-26 with two levels of validation, and possession logic driven by a versioned rules file. Possession counts are compared with pbpstats, a public reference, for every team and game.

KEY DECISION

From 2016-17 the share of identical counts fell from about 98% to about 78%. The cause was clock precision: reading time in whole seconds would restore the match, but would misclassify real possessions. I kept the exact time and documented the difference.

STATUS & NEXT

All 60 season files are loaded, with stints and possessions built and a read-only Streamlit dashboard on top. On/off, lineups and team efficiency are built; next: RAPM.

COURSE PROJECT · STATISTICAL MODELING

What Drives Spotify Track Popularity?

Our MSBA team tested whether audio features such as danceability, energy and loudness explain a track's popularity. I handled data cleaning, hypothesis testing, the regression model and its interpretation. The answer was mostly no: even after removing zero-popularity tracks, the model explained only about 7% of the variation, a sign that popularity depends on factors beyond the audio itself.
