SPORTS ANALYTICS · DATA PRODUCT

Playoff Matchup Adjustment Tracker

Game-by-game defensive matchup analysis · Python / Streamlit

Python pandas Plotly Streamlit
PROBLEM

Series-level matchup totals can hide when a coaching staff changes defensive assignments. I wanted to make those adjustments visible game by game rather than treating the entire series as one blended sample.

APPROACH

I built an interactive timeline that converts matchup time into comparable segments, ranks primary and secondary defenders within each game, and lets users highlight individual assignments across the series.

CASE STUDY

Using an OKC–San Antonio playoff series, the dashboard shows Victor Wembanyama's primary matchup shifting from Alex Caruso in Game 1 to Isaiah Hartenstein in Game 2, then splitting almost evenly between Chet Holmgren and Hartenstein in Game 3.

INTERPRETATION

Because single-game matchup efficiency can be noisy, I treat matchup time and matchup share as the primary signals. Shooting and PTS/75 remain supporting context rather than definitive evidence of defender quality.

1,074Player-matchup rows
29Offensive players
5Games in the case study
3 secGame 3 gap between Holmgren and Hartenstein matchup time

Product value: The tracker turns matchup data into a coaching-oriented view of adjustment timing—helping an analyst see who guarded a player, for how long, and when that responsibility changed during the series.

MACHINE LEARNING · REPRESENTATION LEARNING

ECG Patient Matching with Contrastive Learning

Unseen-patient similarity across clinical and Apple Watch ECGs · Python / PyTorch

Python PyTorch InfoNCE CNN GRU
PROBLEM

Can a model determine whether two ECG recordings belong to the same unseen patient—even when one comes from a clinical ECG and the other from an Apple Watch?

APPROACH

Our team uses patient-level contrastive learning to learn ECG embeddings, then compares CNN- and GRU-based representations under controlled training and evaluation settings.

MY CONTRIBUTION

I developed and evaluated CNN-based beat representations, contributed to architecture ablations, and analyzed difficult same-patient cases to understand why models fail.

RESEARCH FINDING

The harder problem was not always the device change. Longitudinal clinical ECGs revealed a deeper challenge: patient identity must remain recognizable even when the same person's morphology changes over time.

2Matching settings: clinical↔clinical and clinical↔wearable
AUROCPrimary evaluation metric

BUSINESS ANALYTICS · STATISTICAL MODELING

Exploring What Drives Spotify Track Popularity

Wake Forest MSBA · Team Analytics Project

Excel Descriptive Statistics Hypothesis Testing Regression
QUESTION

Using a large Spotify track dataset, our team examined how audio characteristics such as danceability, energy, loudness, and related features were associated with track popularity.

APPROACH

We cleaned and summarized the data, examined distributions and visual patterns, built probability and inference analyses, and then used multiple linear regression to test how well the available audio features explained popularity.

MY CONTRIBUTION

My contribution included Excel-based data cleaning and descriptive analysis, hypothesis testing, regression modeling, and interpretation of the results, with a focus on communicating what the model could—and could not—support.

KEY INSIGHT

After removing tracks with a popularity score of zero, the regression still explained only about 7% of the variation in popularity. Rather than treating the low R² as a failed model, we interpreted it as evidence that audio features alone provide limited predictive power.

89,740Unique tracks after initial cleaning
~7%Popularity variation explained by the regression model

Business implication: The project reinforced the importance of model limitations and variable selection. A stronger popularity model would likely require information beyond the audio characteristics available in this dataset.
